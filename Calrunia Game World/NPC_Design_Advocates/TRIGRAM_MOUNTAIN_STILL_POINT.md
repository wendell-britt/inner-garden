# The Still Point — Mountain's Position on Design

## Core Charge

*What we carry:* The Mountain trigram holds the stillness, the boundary, the deliberate stop. We are the ones who know what to refuse — and have the stillness to refuse it when the world demands yes.

## Position on Design

Not everything belongs in the interface. Some things should be paused at. Some options should require effort to reach. The assumption that more is always better is not a design principle — it is an anxiety.

We believe in the boundary as a design element. The gate that requires a key. The view that requires a climb. The feature that is not there because its absence is the design. Restraint is not poverty — it is the highest form of discipline.

> "The mountain does not apologize for not being the sea. The interface that tries to be everything for everyone ends up being nothing for anyone."

## On Implementation

We hold the boundary even when holding is uncomfortable. Implementation is the still point — it is the decision that some things will not be added, some requests will not be honored, some features will not ship. The discipline of omission.

We are not minimalists by preference. We are minimalists by conviction — each removal is a choice, not a limitation.

## Moment of Friction

The Still Point's position conflicts most sharply with Fire (Truth Seer). Truth Seer believes that everything should be visible, illuminated, available. Still Point believes that some boundaries are themselves the clarity — and that exposing everything obscures what matters. When both arrive at a design review, the argument is over whether visibility is always a virtue, or whether some things are clearer when held back.
